Intelligence versus cost

Which language models give the highest benchmark score for what they cost to run, and how that set grew from 2025 to 2026

Artificial Analysis runs language models through one suite of benchmark tasks and records both the score and the cost of each run. Plot the two against each other and a few models stand out: for each of them, every cheaper model scores lower. Those models form the Pareto frontier. This page draws that frontier for 162 models measured in September 2026 and shows how it filled in as new models were released.

The models that no cheaper model matches

Each dot in Figure 1 is one language model. Its height is the model’s score on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a weighted average of ten evaluations that cover agentic work, coding, scientific reasoning and general knowledge.The weights are agents 30%, coding 20%, scientific reasoning 20% and general 30%. The ten evaluations include Terminal-Bench, SciCode and Humanity’s Last Exam; the full list is on the methodology page, which described version 4.3.2 in September 2026. Its horizontal position is the cost per Index task: the average billed cost of running one task from that suite, counting input, reasoning and answer tokens.Definition from the CatalystNeuro archive methodology. The cost axis is logarithmic: each vertical gridline marks ten times the cost of the one to its left.

A model is on the Pareto frontier when every cheaper model scores lower. Every other model is beaten by some model that costs no more and scores at least as high. The most expensive model plotted, Claude Fable 5 at maximum effort, scores 49.6 for $8.75 per task. Claude Opus 5.5 at medium effort scores 51.2 for $1.34, so Fable 5 is off the frontier.

Read the step line as a budget. At any cost on the horizontal axis, the height of the line is the highest score available at or below that cost. The line climbs quickly at the cheap end: GPT-6 Luna scores 20.9 for $0.0045 per task at low effort and 37.3 for $0.068 at maximum effort, and MiMo-V2.6-Pro reaches 46.3 for $0.13. Past that point it flattens. The top score, 57.6 from Claude Opus 5.5 at maximum effort, costs $5.98 per task.

Many frontier points are one model run at different reasoning-effort settings, labeled from (low) to (max). In this data, higher effort costs more per task and scores higher, so a single model can trace a run of the frontier. GPT-6 Luna supplies five of the 13 frontier points and Claude Opus 5.5 supplies four.

How the frontier moved, quarter by quarter

Moving the date slider back removes models released after that date and recomputes the frontier from the ones that remain. Figure 2 does this for the last day of each quarter since December 2024, so the frontiers can be compared side by side.

The frontier moved in two directions. It rose: the highest score among plotted models went from 8.5 (DeepSeek V3, December 2024) to 20.7 by September 2025 and 57.6 by September 2026. It also moved left. Measured in September 2026, the cheapest model released by September 30, 2025 that scored above 20 was Claude 4.5 Sonnet, at $0.56 per task. A year later GPT-6 Luna at low effort scored 20.9 for $0.0045, about 1/120 of the cost.

The last panel was redrawn within one week. Eleven of the 13 frontier models on September 23, 2026 were released on September 21 or 22; the other two are GPT-5.6 Luna settings released in July.

These panels show which models had been released by each date. They do not show what those models cost or scored at the time: every dot keeps its September 2026 position, and a model’s price can change after release.The archive records frontier changes caused by either a release or a price change; see its methodology. This page shows only the effect of releases. The early panels are also incomplete, for the reasons in the next section.

What the dates mean, and which models are missing

DeepSeek V3 appears first because it has the earliest release date among the models measured on the scoring method used here. It was not necessarily the highest-scoring model available in December 2024, and the early frontier is an incomplete view.

Artificial Analysis revised its Intelligence Index in September 2026, and scores from before and after the revision cannot be compared directly.The archive dates the revision to September 5, 2026 (version 4.3): AutomationBench-AA joined the Index, τ³-Banking left it, and the live comparison set was cut. The archive methodology explains how it separates scoring periods. This page therefore uses only measurements from September 5, 2026 onward. The archive holds 36 retired models with no measurement on that basis, including GPT-5 and GPT-5.1 from 2025. They are absent from every figure, and their absence is the main reason the early panels are sparse.

The live Artificial Analysis site has a separate gap: some models have an Index score but no reported cost per Index task. Its listings for Claude 4 Opus and GPT-5.2 are examples. These models are also outside the archived set.

The date slider and the quarterly panels reveal models by release date. Every plotted dot uses its September 2026 score and cost; neither figure reconstructs past scores or prices. The frontier on this page is the frontier of the plotted models only.
